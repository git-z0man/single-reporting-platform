EUVD statistics

Charts on the EU Vulnerability Database, regenerated every day by the monitor in this repository from the EUVD's own API, the CISA KEV catalogue and Shadowserver honeypot data as served by the EUVD. Data as of 19 Oct 2026. Every panel says what it shows, where the data comes from and what it cannot show. None of it says how a vulnerability reached ENISA: no source records the reporting route. Raw data: stats.json; the exploited entries one by one: index.html; the API: API.md.

This page is pre-rendered: the charts are drawn into the file, so it needs no JavaScript. Hover a bar or dot for its value where your viewer supports it. Each panel can be saved on its own as an image.

Part 3: EU KEV and the SRP

The EU KEV, the catalogue ENISA keeps with the EU CSIRTs Network, against CISA's, and the entries whose dates fit an early notification. None of this shows the reporting route; the background is in EU-KEV.md.

Comparison14. EU KEV against CISA: who lists a vulnerability first

/api/kevEntries/batch (undocumented): the day each catalogue added every entry the EU KEV lists. Shown: the 42 most recent additions, back to 31 Jul 2026.

Scroll sideways for the whole chart.

What you seeOne row per entry, newest at the top. A gold dot is the EU KEV date, a blue dot the CISA date, joined by a grey line; a triangle at the left edge means CISA listed it before the window. In the last 90 days (32 entries in both catalogues) the EU KEV was first for 10, on the same day for 10 and later for 12; the median gap is 0 days. Before that (30 entries) CISA was first for 26, with a median of 283 days: the EU KEV largely added older CISA entries. Rows with only a gold dot are not in CISA's catalogue.

Limits: The EU KEV is not the SRP. Its dates go back to 17 Jan 2025, and no entry carries an EU KEV date in the ten days from the go-live. An entry the EU KEV listed after the go-live and CISA did not, or did later, is a candidate for an SRP origin, nothing more. The date is when the catalogue added the entry, not when the vulnerability was reported. Only the 42 most recent additions are drawn, so the rows stay readable without scrolling; the EU KEV holds 77 entries in all, and the figures above are counted over all of them, not only the rows shown.

Time series19. How urgently CISA wants new entries fixed

CISA KEV catalogue (dateAdded, dueDate, forensicTriage), entries added in the last 24 months, by month.

Scroll sideways for the whole chart.

What you seeEach bar splits a month's new entries by the remediation window CISA set for US federal agencies: 3 days, 7, 14, 21. Hollow dots count entries marked for forensic triage: under CISA's directive BOD 26-04 of 10 June 2026 the agency must fix or mitigate the asset within three days and examine it for signs that it has already been broken into. In the last three full months 78% of new entries got three days. Forensic triage first appears in June 2026 and covers 62% of the entries added since. All 87 marked entries carry the 3-day window, which is what the directive ties the mark to. The 21-day window, the old default under BOD 22-01, last appears in March 2026, before the new directive: the move away from it had already begun, so it is not the triage mark that ended it.

Limits: These are deadlines for US federal agencies, not for anyone in the EU; they show how CISA rates urgency, not how dangerous a flaw is in Europe. The triage mark is a duty to look for a compromise, not a finding that one happened, and it says nothing about what anyone in the EU must do. forensicTriage refers to BOD 26-04, which replaced BOD 22-01 on 10 June 2026, and the field is absent for older entries; the windows shown are the ones in the catalogue, which under the directive an agency may have to shorten or lengthen for its own assets.
