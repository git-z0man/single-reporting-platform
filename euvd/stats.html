EUVD statistics

Charts on the EU Vulnerability Database, regenerated every day by the monitor in this repository from the EUVD's own API, the CISA KEV catalogue and Shadowserver honeypot data as served by the EUVD. Data as of 8 Oct 2026. Every panel says what it shows, where the data comes from and what it cannot show. None of it says how a vulnerability reached ENISA: no source records the reporting route. Raw data: stats.json; the exploited entries one by one: index.html; the API: API.md.

This page is pre-rendered: the charts are drawn into the file, so it needs no JavaScript. Hover a bar or dot for its value where your viewer supports it.

All 401,956 records, not only the exploited ones. Counts come from /api/search with fromDate/toDate (publication date) and the assigner and fromScore filters; the field total gives the count without downloading the records.

Time series5. Known-exploited additions per month

/api/kev/dump (documented): one entry per CVE with its earliest date across CISA KEV and EU KEV, refreshed daily by ENISA.

What you seeEach bar is one month, split by which catalogue lists the CVE; the dashed line marks the SRP go-live month. CISA dominates: 1,674 of 1,747 entries are in CISA's catalogue only, 73 are also or only in the EU KEV. The last full month, September 2026, added 49. The tall bars at the start are CISA building up its catalogue in 2021 and 2022, not surges.

Limits: The month of the earliest date, not of the latest listing. It says what is flagged as exploited, not how it reached ENISA; nothing in this chart can show SRP reporting.

Time series6. Daily additions, recent weeks

The same dump, by day, 15 Aug 2026 to 8 Oct 2026.

What you seeOne bar per day with at least one addition; the dashed line is the SRP go-live (11 Sep 2026) when it falls in the window. 76 additions on 28 of 55 days. Most days without a bar are weekends.

Limits: A day without a bar is a day without additions, not missing data. Daily counts are small and noisy; do not read a trend out of a few days.

Distribution7. How long after publication was an entry flagged?

EUVD record datePublished against exploitedSince, for entries flagged in the last 12 months (309 entries).

What you seeBars count entries by the gap between publication in the EUVD and the day they were flagged as exploited. The largest group is same day (62 entries); 52 waited more than a year. The median gap is 13 days.

Limits: "Before publication" means flagged before the EUVD record existed, usually a record created late. The gap measures when an entry was flagged, not when exploitation began.

Comparison8. Who is faster: the EUVD or the KEV catalogues?

EUVD record datePublished against exploitedSince (the earliest KEV date), for every exploited entry flagged since October 2024 (563 entries), by month of flagging. Months with fewer than 5 entries are left out.

What you seeTop: per month, how many entries were in the EUVD first, on the same day, or flagged by a KEV catalogue first; the EUVD was first for 79%. Bottom: the gap in days, bars for the median and hollow dots for the mean. Over the period the median is 16 days and the mean 475: a few entries waited years, which pulls the mean up, so the median is the fairer "average". Since the go-live (30 entries) the median is 1 and the mean 39, as short as any month in the series.

Limits: The gap runs from the EUVD record to the first KEV listing: how long an entry waited in the EUVD before being flagged, not which source knew first, since a vulnerability may have been public elsewhere earlier. A change in the gap is an observation; the chart cannot say why it changed.

Honeypot9. Honeypot activity per entry: today against the usual level

/api/honeypotObservations/batch (undocumented, used by the EUVD frontend): Shadowserver sensor connections in the last 24 hours and averages over 7, 30 and 90 days.

What you seeEach row is one exploited vulnerability the sensors see attacked, the 16 busiest. The four dots, left to right on a logarithmic scale, are the 90-, 30- and 7-day averages and today (filled); a filled dot far right of the others is a surge. Red rows carry the EUVD's own UPTICK flag (16 of the rows shown; 156 of all 231 entries with counts), grey is steady, green declining.

Limits: Connection counts from sensors, not victims and not the reporting route. Only 330 of 1,747 exploited entries appear in the sensors at all. The EUVD keeps no history of these counts; the daily record started by this monitor is in the last panel.

Coverage10. How much of the exploited list do sensors see?

The same honeypot endpoint, checked for all 1,747 exploited entries, grouped by the year they were flagged.

What you seeBars show the share of entries per year that have honeypot data: 19% overall, highest for 2024 (28%), lowest for 2022 (14%).

Limits: Absence from the sensors does not mean an entry is not attacked, only that the sensors do not emulate it. Edge devices and web applications dominate what sensors see.

Ranking11. Which vendors are flagged most often

EUVD records: the vendor on each exploited entry, by exploitedSince; the last 90 days against the 90 days before.

What you seePairs of bars per vendor: the last 90 days in blue, the 90 days before in grey. Microsoft leads with 12. Newly frequent (at least twice the count of the period before): Fortinet, Linux, JFrog, MikroTik, checkpoint, SonicWall, WordPress. "n/a" is entries without a vendor name.

Scatter12. Severity against likelihood of exploitation

EUVD records of the 105 entries flagged in the last 90 days: CVSS base score and EPSS (the EUVD's own epss field).

What you seeEach dot is one recently flagged vulnerability. Horizontal: CVSS severity. Vertical: EPSS, the modelled chance of exploitation in the next 30 days. 31 entries are critical (CVSS 9+) but had an EPSS under 10% (red): critical flaws the model rated unlikely, yet they are exploited.

Limits: EPSS is a prediction. These entries are already exploited, so low values show misses of the model, not errors in the data. EPSS changes daily; the chart shows the value at the last run.

Change13. How often do exploited entries change?

EUVD records: dateUpdated of the exploited entries per day, last 30 days. Also available through fromUpdatedDate in /api/search.

What you seeEach bar is the number of exploited entries the EUVD edited that day (scores, references, advisories, product data): 153 edits in 30 days, most on 1 Oct 2026 (41).

Limits: Editing activity on the record, not a change in whether something is exploited. A change in the exploited status itself shows up only by comparing daily snapshots, which this monitor keeps.

Comparison14. EU KEV against CISA: who lists a vulnerability first

/api/kevEntries/batch (undocumented): the day each catalogue added every entry the EU KEV lists. Shown: the entries the EU KEV added since 10 Jun 2026.

What you seeOne row per entry, newest at the top. A gold dot is the EU KEV date, a blue dot the CISA date, joined by a grey line; a triangle at the left edge means CISA listed it before the window. In the last 90 days (31 entries in both catalogues) the EU KEV was first for 10, on the same day for 10 and later for 11; the median gap is 0 days. Before that (30 entries) CISA was first for 26, with a median of 283 days: the EU KEV largely added older CISA entries. Rows with only a gold dot are not in CISA's catalogue.

Limits: The EU KEV is not the SRP. Its dates go back to 17 Jan 2025, and no entry carries an EU KEV date in the ten days from the go-live. An entry the EU KEV listed after the go-live and CISA did not, or did later, is a candidate for an SRP origin, nothing more. The date is when the catalogue added the entry, not when the vulnerability was reported.

Time series17. How urgently CISA wants new entries fixed

CISA KEV catalogue (dateAdded, dueDate, forensicTriage), entries added in the last 24 months, by month.

What you seeEach bar splits a month's new entries by the remediation window CISA set for US federal agencies: 3 days, 7, 14, 21. Hollow dots count entries marked for forensic triage. In the last three full months 78% of new entries got three days. Forensic triage first appears in July 2026 and covers 72% of the entries added since.

Limits: These are deadlines for US federal agencies, not for anyone in the EU; they show how CISA rates urgency, not how dangerous a flaw is in Europe. forensicTriage refers to BOD 26-04 and is absent for older entries.

Profile18. Ransomware use and weakness types

CISA KEV catalogue: knownRansomwareCampaignUse by year added, and the cwes field across all 1,734 entries.

What you seeLeft: the share of entries CISA knows to be used by ransomware groups, by year added: 27% in 2021, 11% in 2026. Right: the most frequent weakness types, led by Improper input validation, OS command injection, Out-of-bounds write.

Limits: "Known" ransomware use is added as CISA learns of it, so recent years are low partly because that knowledge has not arrived yet. One entry can carry several weakness types, and older entries often carry none.

euvd/history.jsonl: one line per day written by this monitor, because the EUVD keeps no history of these values: size of the exploited set, size of the EUVD, entries seen by the honeypot sensors and their connections.

What you see2 days recorded, 7 Oct 2026 to 8 Oct 2026. The exploited set went from 1,747 to 1,747 entries; honeypot connections across all entries from 95,708 to 77,543 a day.

Limits: Starts on the day the monitor began recording; nothing before that can be reconstructed. A missing day means the monitor did not run, not that nothing happened.
